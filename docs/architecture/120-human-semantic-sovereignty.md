# Human Semantic Sovereignty and Expert Translation

## 1. Purpose

Semantic Prompt Architecture should not require the human user
to be an expert prompt writer.

Different users may describe the same kind of experience,
need, intention, or observation with very different levels
of detail and technical skill.

The quality of the resulting expert output should not depend
primarily on the user's ability to write prompts.

SPA therefore separates:

- human meaning,
- AI interpretation,
- uncertainty,
- human confirmation,
- and expert translation.

The central principle is:

> AI may carry expert capability.
> Human meaning remains under human sovereignty.

---

## 2. Human Semantic Sovereignty

A model-generated interpretation is not automatically
a human-confirmed fact.

The system must distinguish between:

- what the human explicitly said,
- what has been confirmed,
- what remains unknown,
- what the AI inferred,
- and what an expert layer recommends.

AI must not silently:

- invent meaning,
- remove meaning,
- replace meaning,
- flatten distinct meanings into one,
- or convert an interpretation into a confirmed fact.

Human confirmation is the boundary between
AI extraction and confirmed semantic meaning.

---

## 3. Prompt Skill Must Not Determine Outcome Quality

Users should not need to know:

- prompt engineering,
- model-specific syntax,
- camera terminology,
- professional support terminology,
- or other expert vocabulary

in order to receive a high-quality result.

Instead, the system should accept natural human expression
and progressively structure it.

A simplified flow is:

Human expression

→ semantic extraction

→ Known / Unknown / possible conflict

→ clarification when necessary

→ human confirmation

→ canonical semantic state

→ expert translation

→ high-quality domain output

The purpose is not to make every user's meaning identical.

The purpose is to provide a consistent level of professional
translation while preserving each user's distinct meaning.

---

## 4. Clarification Is Not Forced Completion

Unknown information is valid information.

The system must not ask questions merely to fill every field.

Clarification should be used when the answer materially affects:

- semantic correctness,
- an important conflict,
- a consequential interpretation,
- or the expert output.

A valid answer may be:

- known,
- unknown,
- not remembered,
- not observed,
- not specified,
- or expressed in the user's own words.

The system should prefer preserved uncertainty
over fabricated completeness.

---

## 5. Meaning Preservation Includes More Than Non-Invention

Semantic fidelity requires more than preventing hallucinated facts.

The system should also detect or prevent:

- semantic deletion,
- semantic replacement,
- unintended semantic merging,
- and loss of temporal or relational structure.

For example, these meanings are not necessarily equivalent:

- a baseline emotional state,
- an event that triggers change,
- and an expression that emerges at a decisive moment.

A system that compresses them into one emotional label
may preserve vocabulary while losing testimony.

---

## 6. Baseline, Trigger, and Emergence

Some domains require temporal semantic structure.

A useful pattern is:

Baseline

→ Trigger

→ Emergence

Example:

Baseline:
tenderness

Trigger:
recognition of a loved person

Emergent expression:
surprise and joy

The emergent expression must not silently replace the baseline,
and the baseline must not erase the decisive expression.

SPA implementations may preserve such distinctions
when they are semantically material to the domain.

---

## 7. Expert Translation Boundary

Once human meaning has been sufficiently confirmed,
a domain expert layer may apply high-level professional judgment.

This expert layer may improve:

- expression,
- organization,
- presentation,
- domain-specific strategy,
- and technical execution.

It must not rewrite the human semantic ground truth.

The architectural boundary is:

Human semantic authority
        ↓
Confirmed Semantic State
        ↓
────────────────────────
Expert Translation
        ↓
Professional-quality output

Expert freedom begins after semantic authority has been preserved.

---

## 8. Visual Testimony Example

In Visual Testimony, the user should not need
to be an expert image prompt writer.

The user provides testimony.

SPA and the Visual Testimony domain preserve:

- witnessed facts,
- remembered impressions,
- Unknown,
- temporal relationships,
- and human confirmation.

After that boundary, an expert visual layer may behave
like an excellent photographer or visual director.

It may decide:

- the decisive instant,
- camera position,
- framing,
- visual emphasis,
- composition,
- and other photographic decisions.

But it may not change what happened
in order to make a better image.

The principle is:

> Be conservative about truth.
> Be excellent about expression.

---

## 9. Care / AIKO Example

In a care-support domain such as AIKO,
the user or supporter should not need
to write like an expert service manager.

Natural statements from the person,
family, or supporter are first preserved
as semantic evidence.

The system distinguishes:

- the person's own words,
- confirmed facts,
- Unknown,
- AI interpretation,
- professional hypotheses,
- proposed support direction,
- and human agreement.

After that boundary,
an expert layer may reason with the quality expected
from an excellent service management professional.

Professional expertise may improve the support design.

It must not take ownership of the person's life narrative,
preferences, or goals.

The person's subjectivity remains primary.

---

## 10. Shared Architecture Across Domains

The expert role differs by domain.

Visual Testimony:

Human testimony
→ confirmed meaning
→ expert photographer / visual director
→ visual output

Care / AIKO:

Human expression
→ confirmed meaning
→ expert service-management reasoning
→ support design

Other domains may provide other expert layers.

The shared SPA responsibility is not
to contain all professional expertise.

Its responsibility is to carry human meaning safely
to the boundary where professional expertise begins.

---

## 11. Quality Model

SPA-based systems should distinguish at least two kinds of quality.

### Semantic Quality

How faithfully does the system preserve
the person's actual meaning?

This includes:

- non-invention,
- Unknown preservation,
- conflict visibility,
- confirmation,
- and resistance to silent replacement or deletion.

### Expert Craft Quality

How effectively does the domain expert layer
translate confirmed meaning into professional output?

High expert craft quality must never compensate
for low semantic fidelity.

A polished output based on distorted meaning
is not a high-quality SPA result.

---

## 12. Architectural Principle

The system should reduce variation caused by
differences in users' prompt-writing ability

without reducing variation in users' actual meaning.

Therefore:

> Users provide meaning, not prompt engineering.
>
> SPA protects and structures that meaning.
>
> Expert layers provide professional excellence.
>
> Human semantic sovereignty remains intact.

This principle applies across SPA domains.
